## THREE-TIER LATENT DEMAND MODEL

Fan token market development follows a three-tier latent demand structure.
Agents reasoning about fan token adoption curves should load this model
before assessing market size, conversion potential, or demand signals.

```
TIER 1 — ACTIVE HOLDERS
  Current fan token owners. Engaged with utility layer.
  Smallest tier by population — but proven, converted market.
  Signals: on-chain activity, governance participation, staking behaviour.
  Agent use: baseline engagement benchmark. CDI and FTP frameworks apply.

TIER 2 — AWARE NON-HOLDERS
  Fans who know fan tokens exist but have not yet purchased.
  Largest commercially addressable segment globally.
  Conversion requirements: right moment + right product + right entry point.
  Digital engagement appetite is confirmed — this tier shows consistent
  appetite for interactive sports technology across markets.
  Agent use: near-term demand signal layer. Track activation events
  (tournament entry, club milestone, FTP supply event) as conversion triggers.

TIER 3 — UNAWARE
  Fans with no awareness of fan tokens or digital collectibles.
  Requires brand awareness campaign before product conversion is viable.
  Lower priority for direct fan token demand modelling.
  Agent use: do not model as addressable demand in short-horizon signals.
```

## CROSS-MARKET DIGITAL ENGAGEMENT EXPECTATIONS

Global fan bases consistently indicate that technology and digital
platforms are expected features of the modern sports experience —
not optional enhancements.

```
PRIMARY DEMAND SIGNALS (confirmed cross-market):
  · Interactive live features (real-time prediction, voting, polling)
  · Behind-the-scenes digital access
  · Prediction formats tied to match outcomes
  · Community participation mechanisms

Agent rule: these are structural expectations, not novelty preferences.
Fan token utility design that satisfies these expectations has structural
demand alignment. Utility that diverges from these expectations carries
lower Tier 2 conversion potential.
```

## US MARKET STRUCTURAL CONTEXT

US sports leagues (NBA, NFL, MLB, MLS) carry disproportionate
international fan bases — their reach extends far beyond domestic
stadium capacity or broadcast rights geography.

```
STRUCTURAL GAP:
  The most valuable US sports fan audiences exist in markets that have
  no stadium access, limited broadcast availability, and no traditional
  club engagement products.

  Fan tokens address this gap directly:
    · Location-independent — no stadium required
    · Always-on — not restricted to match calendar
    · Utility-linked — participatory, not passive

Agent rule: for US-listed fan tokens ($CITY, $JUV, $BAR, $PSG),
  international fan base size is a structural demand amplifier.
  Apply this as a positive modifier to Tier 2 addressable demand
  when assessing token demand horizon.
```

## REGIONAL FANDOM PATTERNS — PLAYER-CENTRIC VS TEAM-CENTRIC

Fan bases differ structurally across regions in how they attach
to clubs versus individual players.

```
EUROPEAN MARKETS (general):
  Club identity is primary. Fan loyalty persists through player departures.
  CDI is more stable. Athlete transfer signals have moderate FM1 risk.

ASIA-PACIFIC MARKETS:
  Player-over-team fandom patterns are structurally stronger.
  Club support is often linked to the presence of a specific player.
  Athlete departure in APAC-significant clubs carries higher CDI risk.

AGENT RULE — APAC FM1 MODIFIER:
  When a fan token's club has significant APAC fan base exposure,
  apply elevated FM1 (price-move ≠ club intelligence) scrutiny
  during player transfer windows.

  A price spike during transfer season in a club with high APAC exposure
  may reflect player sentiment, not structural CDI improvement.
  Confirm: is the signal about the club, or about a specific player?
  If player-specific → FM1 applies → briefing only, not CDI update.
```
