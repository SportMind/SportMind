## Legislative History

```
TURKISH CRYPTO REGULATORY FRAMEWORK — EVOLUTION:

TCMB Payment Ban — effective April 30 2021:
  Central Bank of Turkey prohibited use of crypto assets for
  payments of goods and services.
  Trading, holding, and investment remain legal.
  Payment ban remains in force as of 2026-09-28 (re-confirmed this pass).
  Structural context: crypto is investment-only in Turkey —
  not a payment medium. This shapes holder behaviour.

Law 7518 (Capital Markets Law amendment) — effective July 2 2024:
  Primary crypto regulatory framework.
  All Crypto Asset Service Providers (CASPs) must hold SPK
  operating licence to serve Turkish users.
  Article 35/B defines legal framework for crypto assets.
  Ties crypto regulation to existing Capital Markets Law.
  Defines: "crypto asset" · "wallet" · "platform" — same
  meanings as Capital Markets Law.

SPK Communiqué III-35/B.1 — effective March 13 2025:
  Establishment and operational requirements for CASPs.
  Capital adequacy requirements. Governance standards.
  Full licensing deadline: June 2026.

SPK Communiqué III-35/B.2 — effective March 13 2025:
  Working principles and operating standards for CASPs.
  User protection requirements. Reporting obligations.

MASAK Tebliğ (General Communiqué No. 29) — effective June 28 2025:
  CONFIRMED ENACTED (found this pass — was previously described in this
  file only as a September 2025 proposal; it is in fact an earlier,
  already-enacted measure, separate from the freeze-power expansion
  below). Imposes: 48-hour minimum wait between deposit/trade and
  withdrawal (72 hours for a platform's first-time user); daily/monthly
  transfer limits on stablecoins ($3,000/day, $50,000/month, doubling
  possible with enhanced verification); mandatory 20+ character
  transaction descriptions on transfers; enhanced customer-identity and
  transaction-source documentation requirements. Published in the
  Official Gazette, in force from publication date.

MASAK Account-Freeze Power Expansion — proposed September 2025:
  Separate from, and in addition to, the June 2025 Tebliğ above.
  Proposed giving MASAK real-time bank/crypto account monitoring and
  instant freeze authority, expected via the "11th Judicial Package."
  STATUS RE-CHECKED THIS PASS: the 11th Judicial Package was enacted
  in December 2025, but its confirmed content (Tier 2 sources) concerns
  sentencing and prison-transfer provisions, not MASAK freeze powers.
  No source found confirming the MASAK account-freeze expansion was
  included in that package, the 12th Judicial Package (2026, a separate
  civil/judicial-procedure package), or enacted through any other
  vehicle. STATUS: PROPOSED, NOT CONFIRMED ENACTED — do not assume this
  expansion is in force; the June 2025 Tebliğ above is the confirmed,
  enacted MASAK measure.

AK Party Omnibus Bill — introduced March 2 2026:
  Proposed: 10% withholding tax on gains from SPK-licensed
    platforms (quarterly) · president adjustable 0-20%
  Proposed: 0.03% transaction tax on all crypto trades and
    transfers via CASPs
  Proposed: 15-40% income tax on foreign platform users
  Planning and Budget Committee: approved 0.03% transaction
    tax portion (March 4-5 2026)
  Public backlash: mass #kriptodavergiyehayır campaign
    (March 24 2026) — national trending, 24.8M users mobilised

CRYPTO TAX PROVISIONS WITHDRAWN — March 26-27 2026:
  Articles of the Omnibus Bill concerning crypto tax WITHDRAWN from
  parliamentary agenda under Deputy Speaker Celal Adan, by agreement
  between the ruling AK Party and opposition parties (Hurriyet Daily
  News, via Crowdfund Insider — most detailed account found).
  Withdrawal followed negotiations between ruling AK Party
  and opposition parties.
  10% withholding tax: WITHDRAWN
  0.03% transaction tax: WITHDRAWN
  15-40% foreign platform income tax: WITHDRAWN
  AK Party officials stated at the time that a revised version could
  return via a separate legislative proposal.
  No crypto CGT legislation enacted as of March 27 2026.

SECOND SUBMISSION — April 21 2026 (NEW FINDING, not in file before
this pass):
  A new draft omnibus law was received by the Grand National Assembly
  of Türkiye on 21 April 2026, proposing the SAME formal cryptoasset
  taxation framework as the withdrawn March bill: a 0.03% transaction
  tax, a 10% withholding tax on gains, AND a VAT exemption for
  cryptoasset deliveries (this VAT exemption detail was not in the
  March bill's public reporting — genuinely new provision). Source:
  KPMG TaxNewsFlash, a primary tax-advisory publication tracking the
  Turkish Grand National Assembly's own filings directly — this is a
  materially stronger source than the general crypto press coverage of
  the March bill.
  OUTCOME UNCONFIRMED: no source found during this pass — primary or
  Tier 2 — confirms what happened to this April bill afterward. It was
  not found reported as enacted, and not found reported as withdrawn
  again. This is a genuinely open, active situation, not a settled
  "withdrawn, nothing since" one. See Open Questions section below.

CURRENT STATUS — September 2026:
  SPK licensing framework: ACTIVE
  MASAK AML enforcement: ACTIVE (June 2025 Tebliğ confirmed enacted;
    proposed account-freeze expansion NOT confirmed enacted — see above)
  Crypto payment ban: ACTIVE
  Crypto CGT framework: NOT CONFIRMED ENACTED (March bill withdrawn;
    April bill's fate unconfirmed — treat as genuinely open, not as a
    settled "still withdrawn" status)
  Tax on crypto gains: theoretically taxable under general
    income tax law (progressive 15-40%) but no specific
    crypto enforcement mechanism confirmed enacted.
  Direction of travel: legislation has already been resubmitted once
    (April 2026) after the March withdrawal. This is stronger evidence
    of "will return" than the file's prior framing suggested — it is
    not a matter of if but tracking exactly when/whether the April
    attempt (or a further one) succeeds.

## Tax Uncertainty — Unresolved

```
STATUS: UNRESOLVED — no crypto CGT framework confirmed enacted
as of 2026-09-28. This is a MORE ACTIVE unresolved state than the
prior verification found — see the April 2026 resubmission above.

WHAT WAS PROPOSED (March bill, withdrawn March 26-27 2026; April
bill, submitted 21 April 2026, outcome unconfirmed — same substance,
plus a VAT exemption provision not in the March bill's reporting):
  10% withholding tax on gains from SPK-licensed platforms
  0.03% transaction tax on all trades and transfers
  15-40% income tax on foreign platform users (March bill only —
    not confirmed as part of the April bill's specific provisions,
    which KPMG's reporting describes as transaction tax + withholding
    tax + VAT exemption; the foreign-platform income tax component's
    presence in the April bill is UNCONFIRMED, not assumed carried over)

WHAT EXISTS NOW:
  General income tax law: progressive 15-40% on all income
  Theoretically applicable to crypto gains
  No specific crypto enforcement mechanism confirmed enacted
  No clarity on cost basis method, reporting obligations,
    or taxable event definitions for crypto

WHAT IS COMING:
  Legislative reintroduction is no longer merely "likely" — it has
  already happened once (April 21 2026) since the March withdrawal.
  Turkish government has stated intention to tax crypto, and has now
  acted on that intention a second time within the same year.
  Monitor closely for the April bill's outcome — this is the single
  most time-sensitive open item in this file.

AGENT RULE 2 — APPLY UNKNOWN TAX MODIFIER:
  Do not apply any specific CGT rate to Turkish domestic
  holder analysis. Tax framework is UNRESOLVED — and actively so,
  not settled into quiet non-existence.
  Apply UNKNOWN modifier for tax friction component.
  This is distinct from Italy (33% confirmed) and Brazil
  (17.5% operative). Turkey has no rate confirmed enacted.
  When CGT framework is enacted: escalate immediately to
  Strategy & Brainstorm for library update.

AGENT RULE 3 — PTG BURN TAX: STRUCTURALLY UNRESOLVABLE UNTIL CGT
ITSELF EXISTS (reframed this pass):
  Unlike Italy, where a CGT framework exists and only the burn-specific
  disposal treatment is unclear, Turkey has no enacted CGT framework of
  any kind for crypto gains. The question "does a PTG burn constitute
  a taxable disposal" cannot be meaningfully answered in Turkey's case
  because there is no confirmed crypto-specific tax rule to test a
  burn event against in the first place — general income tax law
  (15-40%) theoretically applies to all income, crypto or otherwise,
  but has no crypto-specific enforcement mechanism to apply it through.
  This is a DIFFERENT and more fundamental kind of unresolved than
  Italy's PTG question — Turkey's is a structural gap, not an
  interpretive gap within an otherwise-settled framework.
  Do not conflate the two file's UNRESOLVED statuses as equivalent in
  kind. When Turkey's CGT framework is eventually enacted (via the
  April bill's resolution or a future attempt), THIS is when the PTG
  burn question becomes meaningfully askable for the first time — not
  before.
