## Legislative History

```
RUSSIAN CRYPTO REGULATORY FRAMEWORK — EVOLUTION:

Crypto payment ban — effective April 2020:
  Bank of Russia prohibited use of crypto assets for domestic
  payments of goods and services.
  Ruble remains sole lawful domestic currency.
  Payment ban maintained in Bill 1194918-8 — not lifted.

Law on Digital Financial Assets (DFA Law) — effective January 1 2021:
  First dedicated digital asset legislation in Russia.
  Classified digital financial assets as property.
  Prohibited crypto as a means of payment.
  Did not create a trading framework — grey market persisted.

Crypto Mining Law — signed August 2024:
  Legalised cryptocurrency mining in Russia.
  Permitted international crypto payments through an experimental
  legal regime.
  First step toward formalised crypto framework.
  Russia became world's second-largest bitcoin miner after the
  United States.

Bill 1194918-8 "On Digital Currency and Digital Rights":
  Government submitted: April 1 2026
  First reading: April 21 2026 — 327 of 340 deputies
  Financial Markets Committee revised text: July 8 2026
  Second and third readings (Duma): July 21 2026 — 340/340
  Federation Council: 14-day approval window (~August 4 2026)
  Presidential signature: CONFIRMED — August 5 2026 (signed by Putin,
    reported by TASS)
  Federation Council and presidential signature both completed.
  Core provisions effective: September 1 2026
  Full enforcement (unlicensed platform ban + criminal
    liability): July 1 2027

DIRECTION OF TRAVEL:
  Russia has moved from outright crypto hostility (2017-2020)
  to grudging tolerance (2021-2023) to active legalisation
  (2024-2026). The primary driver is sanctions bypass —
  cross-border trade settlement in crypto formalises what
  grey-market networks were already doing. Domestic crypto use
  remains strictly controlled.
```

## Current Regulatory Framework (effective September 1 2026)

```
CRYPTO PROPERTY STATUS:
  Bill 1194918-8 classifies crypto assets as property (имущество)
  under Russian civil code — not currency.
  Holders receive judicial protection in courts, bankruptcy
  proceedings, and divorce settlements.
  COURT PROTECTION CLAUSE: Law guarantees court protection for
  crypto owners regardless of whether assets were declared prior
  to enactment. Existing holders who never declared assets are
  not penalised — they receive the same judicial protection as
  newly acquired holdings.
  Structural relevance: reduces legal risk for existing Russian
  fan token holders. The threat of retroactive enforcement for
  undeclared holdings is removed by statute.
  This is a structural legitimisation — not liberalisation.

DOMESTIC PAYMENT BAN (MAINTAINED):
  Ruble remains sole lawful currency for domestic transactions.
  Crypto cannot be used for goods and services payments in Russia.
  Advertising crypto for domestic payment use is prohibited.
  Structural holder behaviour modifier: Russian fan token holders
  are investment/speculation only — same constraint as Turkey
  but with additional platform access restrictions.

CROSS-BORDER TRADE SETTLEMENT (LEGALISED):
  Russian companies may use crypto to settle international trade
  transactions — explicit sanctions bypass mechanism.
  This does not affect fan token holder demand directly but
  signals the government's primary motivation for legalisation.

BANK OF RUSSIA LICENSING FRAMEWORK:
  Five licensed participant categories from September 1 2026:
    1. Exchanges (биржи)
    2. Brokers (брокеры)
    3. Custodians/depositories (депозитарии)
    4. Management companies (управляющие компании)
    5. Exchangers (обменники)
  Banks barred from routing payments to unlicensed foreign
  platforms from September 1 2026.
  Full licensing enforcement: July 1 2027 (transition window
  for existing operators).
  Exchange registration requirement: existing crypto exchanges
  must register by July 1 2027.
  Minimum own capital: 15,000,000 rubles.
  Membership of a financial market self-regulatory organisation
  (SRO) required as condition of registration.

INVESTOR ACCESS RESTRICTIONS:
  Non-qualified retail investors:
    Annual purchase cap: 300,000 rubles per year per licensed
    intermediary (~$3,800 at prevailing rates)
    Knowledge test REQUIRED before any purchase — applies to
    both retail and qualified investors
    Single licensed intermediary only
  Qualified investors:
    No annual purchase limit
    Broader platform access · knowledge test still required
  Structural note: 300,000 ruble cap is a hard per-intermediary
  ceiling, not a lifetime cap. A holder may use multiple licensed
  intermediaries but each carries its own 300,000 ruble annual limit.
  Fan token holders: if fan tokens are eligible assets
  (UNRESOLVED — see below), non-qualified Russian investors
  face structural demand suppression via the 300,000 ruble cap.

ASSET ELIGIBILITY: UNRESOLVED — CRITICAL GAP
  Bill 1194918-8 does not name eligible crypto assets.
  Bank of Russia determines asset eligibility via implementing
  regulations — due October-November 2026.
  Privacy-focused coins excluded on AML grounds.
  Bitcoin and Ethereum widely cited as likely first approvals.
  Fan tokens (Chiliz Chain utility tokens): eligibility
  UNRESOLVED — not named, not excluded, not confirmed.
  This is the most consequential unknown for SportMind: if fan
  tokens are ineligible, Russian residents cannot legally access
  them via licensed platforms from September 1.

DIGITAL RUBLE (PARALLEL LAUNCH):
  Russia launches digital ruble at major banks and retailers
  September 1 2026 — same date as crypto framework.
  Digital ruble is a CBDC — separate from crypto assets.
  Signals Russia's broader digital currency strategy.
  Not directly relevant to fan token analysis but provides
  structural context for Russian digital asset policy.

CGT / TAX FRAMEWORK:
  No specific crypto capital gains tax rate confirmed in
  Bill 1194918-8 — tax provisions left to Federal Tax Service
  implementing guidance.
  General income tax rates (13-15% for individuals) theoretically
  apply to crypto gains as property income.
  No specific enforcement mechanism for fan token gains confirmed.
  Apply UNKNOWN modifier for Russian domestic holder tax friction.

SPORTMIND MODIFIER — RUSSIAN-RESIDENT HOLDERS:
  RUSSIA: asset eligibility UNRESOLVED · domestic payment ban
  active · R$300,000 annual cap (non-qualified) · EU sanctions
  restrict Western platform access · CGT UNKNOWN · September 1
  framework — verify Bank of Russia asset list before analysis
```

## Bidirectional Platform Access Restrictions

```
DIRECTION 1 — RUSSIAN LAW RESTRICTS OUTBOUND ACCESS:
  From September 1 2026: banks barred from routing payments
  to unlicensed foreign platforms.
  Socios.com / Chiliz: not a Bank of Russia-licensed CASP.
  Russian residents routing payments to Socios.com via Russian
  banks may face bank-level blocking from September 1.
  Self-custody (Chiliz Chain wallets): not prevented by the
  payment routing restriction but asset eligibility on licensed
  platforms remains UNRESOLVED.
  Non-qualified investors: R$300,000 annual cap applies to
  purchases through licensed intermediaries — unlicensed
  platform access does not exempt from this restriction.

DIRECTION 2 — EU SANCTIONS RESTRICT INBOUND ACCESS:
  EU 20th sanctions package (effective May 2026): EU residents
  prohibited from transacting with Russian crypto service
  providers.
  Socios.com / Chiliz: EU-licensed (MFSA Malta authorisation —
  MiCA passporting). Cannot legally serve Russian-resident
  users under EU sanctions framework.
  Western-licensed fan token platforms are structurally blocked
  from Russian user onboarding.
  This is a demand signal modifier: Russian fan token demand
  cannot easily flow through mainstream Western platforms.

COMBINED STRUCTURAL EFFECT:
  Russian-resident fan token holders face restrictions from
  both directions simultaneously:
  · Russian law blocks payment routing to unlicensed foreign
    platforms and caps investment via licensed ones
  · EU sanctions prevent Western platforms from serving
    Russian users
  This makes Russia the most structurally isolated holder
  jurisdiction currently documented in SportMind.
  Do not model Russian domestic demand as equivalent to other
  high-friction jurisdictions (Italy · Brazil · Turkey) —
  the access restriction layer is qualitatively different.

AGENT RULE 2 — BIDIRECTIONAL ACCESS RESTRICTION:
  Apply bidirectional access restriction modifier to all
  Russian-resident fan token holder analysis.
  Russian law + EU sanctions = structural platform isolation.
  Russian holder demand signals for any Chiliz fan token
  carry elevated uncertainty and access friction not present
  in any other jurisdiction in the SportMind library.
```
